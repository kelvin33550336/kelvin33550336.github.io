Research

General relativity has passed every test so far, but the most extreme environments in the Universe, such as the near-horizon region of a rapidly spinning black hole and when the moment two black holes merge, are exactly where a beyond general relativity signature might first show itself. My research aims to make precise, testable predictions for that regime and to confront them with gravitational-wave data.

Rapidly rotating black holes beyond Einstein

Modified theories such as dynamical Chern–Simons, scalar Gauss–Bonnet and axidilaton gravity predict corrections to the Kerr metric, but until recently those corrections were known only for slowly rotating black holes. Using pseudospectral methods, we solve the field equations directly at any spin and repackage the result as compact, closed-form metrics accurate to roughly one part in 108 up to spins of 0.99. This shows that the traditional slow-rotation expansions fail already at moderate spins. Pushing to the extremal limit, we find that dynamical Chern–Simons gravity admits a regular extremal black hole, while scalar Gauss–Bonnet gravity develops a horizon singularity, which sharpens where the effective-field-theory description can be trusted.

Quasinormal modes and black-hole spectroscopy

A perturbed black hole resonates at a characteristic set of frequencies, its quasinormal modes. Within the METRICS framework we compute these frequencies for spinning black holes in modified gravity, and provide fitting formulae ready for data analysis. Using rapidly rotating metric solutions that I developed, we have also extended scalar quasinormal-mode calculations to spins as high as 0.99, where corrections can grow by orders of magnitude.

Papers: [4], [7]

Neutron stars with dark matter

Universal "I-Love-Q" relations link a neutron star's moment of inertia, tidal deformability and quadrupole moment almost independently of the nuclear equation of state. Solving the Einstein-Klein-Gordon system for fermion-boson stars, we showed how a bosonic dark-matter component can break these relations, by up to about 5% for light bosons, and identified when they continue to hold.

Papers: [3]

Differentiable waveforms for data analysis

As an undergraduate I helped build ripple, a JAX implementation of gravitational-wave waveform models that runs on GPUs and supports automatic differentiation. Using this package, I recalibrated all fitting coefficients of the IMRPhenomD model against numerical-relativity simulations at once, which roughly halved the median mismatch, with the biggest gains for spinning binaries.
