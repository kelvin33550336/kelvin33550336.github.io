Hi, I'm Kelvin.

I am a PhD student in the Department of Physics at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, working with Prof. Nicolás Yunes on gravitational physics. I use black holes and gravitational waves as laboratories for testing Einstein's theory of general relativity in its most extreme regime.

Much of my recent work develops spectral methods to construct rotating black holes in theories beyond general relativity, delivering compact, closed-form metrics that remain accurate all the way to extremal spins, and then using them to compute quasinormal modes and confront the predictions with gravitational-wave observations. Before Illinois, I received my B.Sc. in Physics from The Chinese University of Hong Kong (with an exchange semester at UC Berkeley), where I worked on differentiable gravitational-wave waveforms and on neutron stars with dark-matter admixtures.

Research interests

  • Modified gravity & EFTRotating black holes in quadratic gravity (dCS, sGB, axidilaton), extremal limits, and the regime of validity for effective field theories.
  • Black hole perturbation theoryQuasinormal modes, black-hole spectroscopy and tests of general relativity with gravitational waves.
  • Numerical & spectral methodsHigh-accuracy pseudospectral solvers that turn field equations into closed-form, approximate metrics.
